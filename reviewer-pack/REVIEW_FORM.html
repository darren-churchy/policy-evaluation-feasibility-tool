Thank you for looking at this. It is a decision-support tool for choosing an impact evaluation design: it asks what constraints apply, then says which designs are ruled out, which remain possible and what would have to be established to use them. It ranks designs by the assumption that makes their comparison valid and scores nothing.

You are not expected to answer everything. Section 1 is the part worth your time if you read nothing else. Everything is optional and nothing you leave blank is recorded as agreement — the output carries only what you actually answered.

1. The decisions the tool rests on

These are the ones worth your time if you read nothing else. Each is currently guarded by a test, so disagreeing with one means changing the tool deliberately rather than by accident.

Designs should be ranked by the assumption that makes their comparison valid, not by the sophistication of the estimator.
The five rungs, highest first:
  • Tier undefined Randomised — Random allocation gives exchangeability: treated and untreated groups differ only by chance, in measured and unmeasured characteristics alike.
  • Tier undefined Local as-if random — An assignment rule or instrument isolates exogenous variation — but only where its own conditions hold, and those conditions are themselves testable claims.
  • Tier undefined Comparison-group panel — Parallel trends — the treated and comparison groups would have moved together absent the intervention — or weighted comparison units that tracked the treated unit before it.
  • Tier undefined Selection on observables — No unmeasured confounding given the measured covariates. Untestable in principle; the argument rests on substantive knowledge of the assignment process.
  • Tier undefined Single time series — The pre-intervention trend would have continued unchanged. No comparison group exists to falsify this, so it stands or falls on the plausibility of the counterfactual trend.
A design should take its worst state across its checks, so no good answer offsets a bad one.
A design with one blocker is blocked however well everything else is met. The alternative — averaging, or trading data quality against design strength — is what the previous tool did.
An unanswered question should leave a design Unknown, never feasible by default.
The previous tool scored blanks at mid-range, so a recommendation appeared before anything had been answered.
Not having checked something should never rule a design out.
Overlap, density at a cutoff and first-stage strength cannot be established before the data exist. Answering "not yet established" raises a condition with something to go and do; only an actual adverse finding blocks.
The theory-based strand should run beside the ladder and never be converted into a rung.
Covering Contribution analysis, Realist evaluation, Process tracing.
The register should hold one row per design, not per estimator.
Estimator choice never moves a design up a tier, so Callaway-Sant’Anna and Sun-Abraham are options of staggered difference-in-differences rather than designs in their own right.
Contested placement: Regression discontinuity in time (RDIT) sits at Tier 3.
Tier 3 chosen
Identification rests on a continuity assumption across a date, which is a time-series claim: nothing else changed at the cutoff. That is the same class of assumption as a comparison-group panel, not local randomisation.
Tier 2 — where a sharp administrative cutoff in time is externally imposed
Treated as a discontinuity design proper, on the grounds that the running variable is a genuine assignment rule.
Contested placement: Controlled interrupted time series sits at Tier 3.
Tier 3 chosen
The control series supplies a comparison group, placing it with the panel designs rather than with as-if-random assignment.
Tier 2
Sometimes grouped with quasi-experimental designs of higher internal validity where the control series is strongly comparable.
Contested placement: Matching and weighting with prior-outcome controls sits at Tier 4.
Tier 4 — upper chosen
Still selection on observables — conditioning on prior outcomes narrows the scope for unmeasured confounding but does not remove it.
Tier 3
Where pre-period outcomes are used and comparability is demonstrated, the Maryland scale allows level 3, which maps to Tier 3.
